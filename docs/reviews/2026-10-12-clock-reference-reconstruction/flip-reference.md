## Tile construction, material, depth, and light

**Observed:** Tiles are muted dark olive, approximately portrait rectangles. Each has a continuous rounded silhouette and a subtle light rim along its top, with a darker narrow inner edge. The face varies gently in tone and has a faint fine-grained/mottled appearance. The upper and lower portions belong to the same material family; neither is a dramatically different color. There is a short soft cast shadow, most apparent below the tiles, and a narrow light separation around the silhouette.

**Observed layer order:** Pale background behind soft cast shadow; rounded tile body/rim; two face portions separated by the middle seam; cream numerals on those faces; small hinge/tab components at the lateral ends of the seam. The digits are interrupted by the physical seam, whose fine light/dark line remains visible through the number area.

**Observed corner/edge profile:** Corner radius is roughly 0.11–0.13 of tile width. Corners are rounded rectangles, with straight vertical side walls and long straight horizontal top/bottom runs. They are neither pill shapes nor sharp squares. The rim is narrow relative to the face, on the order of 1–3 reference pixels, rather than a heavy border.

**Inference:** This reads as a thin, somewhat rigid flip-clock plate with a shallow bevel, matte/satin surface, and gentle light from above/left. The image alone cannot resolve whether the modeled substrate is painted metal, plastic, or another material. A shiny glass/acrylic interpretation is weak: no strong reflections or transparent edges are visible.

**Concrete checks:** At frame scale, a reviewer should identify a tile edge, face, and shadow without needing a thick outline. The face must remain the largest visually quiet region. A broad dark gradient, large inner shadow, or uniform capsule-like outline would change the target construction. Shadows should establish a small distance from the background, not floating slabs or a deeply extruded device.

## Seam and hinge geometry

**Observed:** Each tile has one horizontal seam across its midpoint, at approximately 0.51 tile height. It is a very thin dark line accompanied by a restrained light edge. The seam is level across both tiles. Small vertical rectangular hinge/tab forms sit at the left and right tile edges where that seam terminates. Each is about 0.035–0.045 tile width and 0.12–0.14 tile height. They have dark edge/center shading and tiny highlights; they are not circles or large protruding cylinders.

**Observed:** The seam goes through the numerals near their vertical middle. The cream glyph shapes remain aligned above and below it; there is no conspicuous horizontal shift, duplicate digit, or gap that splits the number into disconnected floating halves.

**Uncertainty / alternate reading:** The concept is small enough that the hinge forms could be short dark fastening plates or exposed axle end pieces. Their exact mechanical assembly is unresolved. What is visible and robust is their short vertical rectangular silhouette, edge placement, symmetry, and local contrast. The seam could be a fine opening with a lit lower edge rather than a broad recessed groove. Re-examination favors the fine-opening reading: its visual weight is very small compared with a digit stroke.

**Concrete checks:** Seam should be substantially thinner than the numeral strokes and run through the full face width. Hinges must visually connect to seam ends. The center gap must stay open rather than be bridged by a thick continuous axle. Do not invent screw heads, rivets, broad central bars, or elaborate mechanisms as defining details.

**Inference:** Directional shading and unequal visible widths imply leaves rotating in space. The source does not justify an exact botanical species, exact leaf count, or elaborate vein anatomy.

**Concrete checks:** A plant substitute should preserve a rising main branch plus a lower leftward fan, alternation, varied angles, varied face widths, and negative space. Veins should be subtle internal marks that follow curved leaves; they should not become dark decorative outlines. Tallest foliage should feel attached through continuous stems to the vase opening.
