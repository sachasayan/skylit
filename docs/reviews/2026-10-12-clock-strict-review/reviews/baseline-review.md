# Blind baseline review — Linen light

Reviewed the four size sheets and all 28 individual native crops in `baseline/`, using the locked `rubric.md`. No implementation source, repository reports, history, or earlier critic verdicts were inspected. The seven supplied shapes are minimum 3×3, wide 6×4, medium 6×6, tall 3×12, strip 12×3, full 12×12, and near-square 6×10.

**Verdict: the baseline does not yet pass the approved concept-quality gate.** The layouts generally read clearly and several adaptations are sensible, but the large native crops expose consequential material and illustration gaps. A lack of clipping is not an aesthetic pass.

## Prioritized visible corrections

### P1 — Redraw the foliage, pots, and shelves at the quality their prominence demands

**Analog: wide, medium, full, near-square.** The leaves are swollen oval forms with blunt/polygonal contours and repeated directions. At full size they look like chunky folded cutouts, rather than the reference's lighter trailing vine. The pot's narrow, hard sloping silhouette and nearly rectangular shelf reinforce the generic ornament impression. Near-square makes this particularly conspicuous because the branch is large and pushed against the right edge.

Use smoother pointed leaf contours with more varied size, angle, curvature, and overlap; slender flowing stems; restrained vein and leaf-face shading. Make a believable fuller terracotta vessel with a clear lip, curved wall/base, warm volume, and contact shadow. Give the shelf a shallow wooden top/front and controlled edge light. The solution need not become photorealistic, but the visible shapes and modeling must feel carefully illustrated. Keep the plant's current supporting role and allow a deliberate edge crop; no need to copy the reference's coordinates.

**Botanical: wide, medium, tall, full, near-square.** The large sprig has only a few broad, densely packed blades. The stiff central line, abrupt leaf junctions, large faceted faces, and nearly repeated paired arrangements make it heavy and mechanical. Full and near-square are the clearest evidence; tall shows the same problem despite the appropriate bottom placement.

Redraw this as an airy branch with flowing thin stems, more natural direction changes, clean tapering tips, modest curvature, and varied broad/narrow leaves. Reduce the visual density around the lower cluster. Add fine tonal/vein variation without thick outlines or conspicuous folds. Preserve the warm serif clock's dominance. Do not add foliage back to minimum or strip merely to prove fidelity; their omission is appropriate.

**Flip: wide, medium, full, near-square.** Its sprig repeats the same heavy leaf construction, while the vessel reads as a small flat teardrop and the shelf as a bar. Full reveals the absence of convincing vase volume most clearly.

Use a lighter, finer branching sprig and a pale ceramic vessel with a coherent neck, shoulder, base, soft directional shading, and contact shadow. Give the shelf the same restrained material finish as Analog. Keep the ornament subordinate and its generous breathing room.

### P1 — Give Flip panels tangible material and a more convincing joint

**All Flip shapes; inspect full and near-square first.** The panels currently read mainly as softly shaded olive rounded rectangles with a thin horizontal line. There is some gradient and external shadow, but their upper/lower faces are insufficiently distinguished. The seam does not suggest a recessed joint, and the pale edge hinges feel applied rather than seated in a mechanism. This is a finish failure even where foliage is absent, including tall and strip.

Introduce restrained upper/lower tonal and light differences, a very small recessed seam shadow/highlight, a credible rounded panel edge/thickness, and integrated hinge recesses. A subtle nonrepeating matte surface variation would help. Keep all of this gentle: the reference is warm and tactile, not shiny plastic. Preserve the strong ivory digits and their current clear reading order. Tune the seam so it remains secondary to the digits.

### P1 — Give Landscape an illustrated surface and coherent light

**All Landscape shapes; full and near-square show the gap most clearly.** The olive and terracotta bands are large unmodulated fields, while the sun is a crisp simple disc. The silhouette system is readable, but the scene does not yet have the reference's softly textured painted/paper character. Wide and strip make the art island's upright left edge and stiff first hill especially visible; tall magnifies the sun into a conspicuously plain shape.

Add low-contrast, fine paper/paint variation and broad gentle tonal/light modulation to the hills and warm sky. Give the sun a softer luminous character, avoiding a harsh rim. Refine the wide/strip left hill entrance so the curve meets the split composition gracefully instead of looking like an abruptly cut standalone block. Retain smooth layered overlaps and calm horizons. The existing serif time, hierarchy, aspect-ratio-specific landscape allocation, and intentional negative space should remain.

### P2 — Rebalance Analog strip as one information group

**Analog strip only.** In the 1007×142 crop, the dial is around x300–418 and the date around x426–494. Their combined group centers around x397, while the card center is x503. The much larger empty area on the right lacks a compositional reason; the result looks parked left rather than deliberately placed.

Center the combined dial/date group as a unit, with its existing readable date relationship, or establish another clear balanced alignment. This is not a request to fill the entire strip: the small round dial naturally follows the available height, and calm side space is appropriate when balanced.

### P3 — Strengthen Botanical's type distinction when refining finish

**Most visible in full and near-square.** The present serif is elegant and the scale is confident, but it is noticeably finer and more Times-like than the reference's fuller warm terracotta display. It also closely shares Landscape's numeral language. A modestly stronger display weight and a slightly warmer copper hue would better establish Botanical's own identity. This is a refinement, not an independent blocker in the foliage-free minimum and strip crops.

## Successful choices to retain

- Large serif time in Landscape and Botanical is readable and visually confident. There is no general timid-type blocker in those concepts.
- Landscape's dates and AM/PM remain secondary; the tall variant's quiet sky is purposeful. Do not consume that space merely to imitate the square reference.
- Analog's tall dial-only layout is a valid calm composition. The dial and date remain legible; missing decoration there is acceptable.
- Flip's tall stack is an intentional response to its narrow shape. The digits remain easy to read and the labels are well attached.
- Botanical's asymmetric background shape and left alignment are valid adaptations. The organic background does not need to match the reference's exact blobs.
- No clipping, unreadable time, or label collision was observed in the supplied native crops. This does not establish refresh, animation, or time correctness.
