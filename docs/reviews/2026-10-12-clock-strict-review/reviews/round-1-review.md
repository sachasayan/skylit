## Actionable remaining blockers

### P1 / A1 — Analog vine lacks convincing botanical contours

**Affected: wide, medium, full, near-square.** Full and near-square provide the clearest native evidence. Several large leaves have nearly straight upper edges, blunt squared-off tips, abrupt planar corners, and rigid filled silhouettes. The larger upper-left and upper-right leaves are especially conspicuous. Fine stems and vein detail do not overcome these coarse primary contours. The result reads as a schematic leaf cutout rather than the reference's delicate trailing plant.

Redraw the leaf silhouettes themselves. Use varied, smooth heart/teardrop forms appropriate to a vine, with a definite natural tip, controlled concavity at the stem, and softly curved edges. Vary leaf turn, overlap, and shape rather than attaching repeated broad plaques. Apply quiet light/shadow across the leaf faces to suggest a slight curve, avoiding a single hard fold. Keep the pot, shelf, trailing direction, and current ornament allocation; this is an illustration correction, not a layout rebuild. Check full and near-square before judging the small crops.

### P1 / B1 — Botanical branch is too mechanical and heavy

**Affected: wide, medium, tall, full, near-square.** In full, the stem remains largely straight while the lower leaves form broad pointed wedges with abrupt corners and dense overlaps. Bare side lines protrude conspicuously beyond several leaf bases. Tall and near-square show the same stiff cluster even at smaller scale. Surface grain and fine veins supply detail, but the plant's silhouette still lacks the reference's airy curves and graceful varied foliage.

Draw a slightly curving central stem and naturally curved secondary stems. Attach leaves cleanly without exposed linear stubs. Give each leaf a more organically convex outline, curved tip, and distinct angle/length/width; open the lower cluster so it does not read as stacked triangular wedges. Use gentle leaf-face modeling rather than relying on the grain/veins for all depth. Preserve the large warm serif time and asymmetrical background; minimum and strip may continue omitting foliage.

### P1 / F1 — Flip ornament remains a paper silhouette rather than a modeled sprig and ceramic vase

**Affected: wide, medium, full, near-square.** Full is the strongest evidence. The sprig has hard pointed polygonal edges and abrupt leaf joins. The pale vessel's broad pear shape has almost no directional light or side/base definition; its visible surface reads as textured cream paper. The shelf has sufficient restrained thickness for its small role, but it cannot make the vase look volumetric.

Smooth and vary the sprig's leaf contours and curvature, retain fine branching, and give the foliage restrained leaf-face shading. Model the vase with a quiet lit side, shaded turning side, believable curved shoulder/neck/base, and a small contact shadow on the shelf. Avoid dark outlines or glossy plastic effects. The vessel should retain its pale ceramic character and modest size. No need to enlarge the decoration or move it closer to the numerals.

The panels themselves are not an independent blocker in this round's images: their restrained bevel, soft shadow, fine matte surface, visible seam, and integrated edge hardware establish plausible physical objects at native scale. Digits remain confident and legible, including the tall stack and compact strip.

### P2 / L1 — Landscape side transition looks like an artificial blurred curtain

**Affected: wide and strip only.** At the left edge of the artwork, a conspicuous vertical column blurs/fades both terracotta and olive layers into the clock's background. Wide shows this at roughly the middle of the card; strip repeats it before its long scene. It reads as a soft mask applied to a separately cropped illustration, not as a natural feature of the warm painted landscape. The scene and type allocation can remain split, but this visible treatment is insufficiently finished.

Replace the vertical mask with clean intentional geometry. For example, begin the foreground near the bottom at the left of the scene and let a smooth low crest rise to the right, using the same crisp-but-softly-lit painted edge treatment as the other hills. Alternatively, carry a low landscape sweep across the base with a quiet land entrance below the time. Preserve the balanced text/art allocation and the strong numerals. A more intense blur is not the correction.

The other five Landscape crops pass: the smooth layered curves, fine surface variation, warm sun, restrained olive/terracotta modeling, and clear serif hierarchy form a convincing warm illustrated scene. The deliberately large cropped sun and calm sky in tall are acceptable adaptations.
