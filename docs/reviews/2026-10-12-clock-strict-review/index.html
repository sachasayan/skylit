Original review text

# Locked clock concept review rubric

Established from `approved.png` alone, before viewing implementation captures. Only Analog (top-left), Landscape (top-middle), Flip (top-right), and Botanical (bottom-left) are in scope. Ring and Window are excluded. This rubric remains fixed for the first implementation review.

## Acceptance rule

Every selected concept must pass two independent gates in every supplied shape:

1. **Concept quality and aesthetic finish:** the implementation convincingly carries the reference's defining visual language, with finished typography, materials, lighting, art, curves, hierarchy, ornament, and warmth.
2. **Responsive composition and readability:** the particular shape has a deliberate, balanced composition and readable clock information. It must feel designed rather than compressed or merely made to fit.

Passing one gate cannot compensate for failing the other. Functional correctness (accurate time, valid labels, absence of clipping) is evaluated separately from fidelity and finish. There is no credit for improvement over an earlier implementation.

The square reference supplies visual language, not a mandatory layout. Dates may move, panels may stack, hills may be redrawn, and ornaments may shrink or be omitted when the resulting composition preserves the concept and its quality. Calm negative space is a positive feature when it supports hierarchy and balance; it is not a defect merely because the reference uses that space differently.

## Shared visual language

- **Warmth:** luminous ivory and cream surfaces, olive foliage and dark olive type, restrained copper/terracotta accents. The palette feels sunlit and domestic, with controlled tonal contrast rather than harsh black/white or saturated accent color.
- **Surface and lighting:** a soft, tangible paper/ceramic-like ground, subtly modulated rather than a sterile flat fill. Rounded containers have credible gentle edge light and shallow soft shadows. Depth remains restrained and coherent; bevels, texture, gradients, and shadows should reinforce an implied material instead of appearing as unrelated effects.
- **Curves:** generous clean corner radii, smooth hills and organic background shapes, delicate tapering foliage. Curves must be controlled and graceful, without mechanical kinks, blunt joins, or arbitrary bulges.
- **Hierarchy:** time or the analog face commands the composition. Date and AM/PM are distinctly secondary, with spacious tracking and sufficient contrast. Decorative art supports the clock without competing for its reading area.
- **Numeral scale:** display numerals are confident and substantial. Reducing type to a timid label, or filling a shape so aggressively that it loses breathing room, both undermine the reference. Numerals need balanced proportions, controlled spacing, and a type family that supplies the selected concept's character.
- **Ornament proportion:** the plants, shelves, and landscape layers are thoughtfully cropped and proportioned. They retain enough quality to merit their visual prominence. Decorative size should follow the composition, not a requirement to reproduce a fixed bounding box.

## Analog defining qualities

- A large, luminous circular dial integrated with the warm surface. Its shallow rim and gentle shading imply a real disc with soft depth; it should not read as a plain outline pasted onto a rectangle.
- Slender, warm brown cardinal numerals and restrained hour marks, with generous spacing around the face. Their scale makes the dial effortlessly readable without making the numerals heavy.
- Copper hour/minute hands with softly rounded ends and believable slight thickness, a small olive pivot, and a fine olive seconds hand. Proportions and hand contrast produce a composed, recognizable clock.
- The plant at the lower-right is a delicate trailing vine with varied leaves, fine stems, subtle leaf shaping/veins, and a believable warm terracotta pot. The wooden shelf has material and shallow depth. If retained, it must feel illustrated with care, rather than a stock icon or coarse repeating leaf glyph.
- Decoration may be smaller or omitted in constrained shapes, provided the remaining dial and material treatment still give the concept its warm tactile character.

Consequential gaps include a lifeless flat dial, heavy or generic marks, poorly proportioned hands, an oversized pivot, or conspicuous crude plant/pot/shelf art.

## Landscape defining qualities

- Large, elegant serif display time in dark olive. The reference's fine/thick stroke contrast and broad, poised numeral shapes are central to its character; generic small sans-serif digits would lose the concept.
- A small, widely tracked uppercase date above, with a separate restrained AM/PM below. The clock occupies the quiet sky with ample breathing room.
- A warm cream/peach sky, a softly glowing ochre sun, and smoothly layered rolling hills in terracotta and olive. The layers have gentle texture and tonal variation, giving them a paper/painted character instead of generic flat clip art.
- The landscape is low and calm, with measured asymmetry and believable overlaps. Hill silhouettes and sun placement must create an intentional scene rather than a decorative stripe or arbitrary overlapping ovals.
- Hills may be redrawn for each aspect ratio and the date/period may move. Retain confident typography, warm depth, and a composed relationship between sky and land.

Consequential gaps include timid numerals, weak serif character, stiff or arbitrary hill curves, a harsh flat sun, conspicuously generic art, or loss of the quiet sky/time hierarchy.

## Flip defining qualities

- Two substantial dark olive flip panels with warm ivory, large clean sans-serif numerals. The numeral weight, scale, centering, and open shapes carry the immediate readability of the concept.
- Rounded panel corners, restrained upper/lower tonal differentiation, a precise midline seam, and small edge hinges express a plausible physical mechanism. Shallow highlights, shadows, and surface variation give the panels weight and thickness.
- The seam is subordinate to the digits; hinges are small convincing details, not large visual interruptions. Panel spacing is narrow and deliberate, and the pair reads as one clock.
- AM/PM is secondary, widely tracked, and visually attached to the panel group.
- The lower-right olive sprig in a softly modeled pale ceramic vase and warm wood shelf adds quiet domestic detail. Its leaves are varied and tapered, the stems fine, and the vase has a credible neck/base and softly shaded volume. If retained, it must be more than a coarse icon.
- Stacking panels is allowed when it yields a purposeful compact composition. The resulting group still needs balanced numeral scale, coherent material depth, and a clear reading order.

Consequential gaps include flat featureless panels, flimsy panel proportions, shallow or generic digits, oversized mechanical details, miscentered halves, or coarse vase/sprig art.

## Botanical defining qualities

- Very large warm terracotta serif display numerals with confident weight and rounded, elegant contours. This is a strong typographic composition, not a small clock with a plant added.
- Small widely tracked olive weekday/date text, and a secondary AM/PM. Alignment and spacing create a calm editorial hierarchy.
- Soft, translucent organic cream/beige shapes add subtle depth to the ground. Their asymmetry and curvature feel natural and composed, not like random overlapping circles or a conspicuous unrelated badge.
- A delicate olive branch rises from the lower-right. It uses thin curving stems, varied pointed leaves, graceful angle changes, and subtle leaf veins/tonal modeling. The branch is airy and botanically expressive, rather than thick, blunt, symmetrical, or mechanically repeated.
- Foliage acts as a substantial but subordinate counterweight to the time. It may be reduced, simplified carefully, or omitted in a constrained shape, but the warm typographic and organic-surface identity must remain convincing.

Consequential gaps include timid or characterless time type, overly dark/heavy foliage, coarse leaf geometry, visually dominant background blobs, or a generic unmodulated background that loses the reference's softness.

## Responsive gate

For every supplied aspect ratio, inspect:

- Time is readable first, with useful display scale, sufficient contrast, and balanced spacing. Analog hand/dial readability and Flip digit order remain clear.
- Date and AM/PM retain a distinct secondary relationship and comfortable distances; they neither float accidentally nor collide with numerals/art.
- The clock group is intentionally positioned within the shape. Visual weight, margins, and negative space form a stable whole, including when decoration is absent.
- Ornament and illustration are proportioned and cropped with purpose. No near-tangencies, accidental edge cuts, cramped leaves, trapped labels, or residual fragments from an unsuitable square layout.
- Materials and art survive the new scale. Tiny details may be removed, but remaining shapes, seams, curves, and textures must still be clean and expressive.
- Wide, square, and narrow variants may use different arrangements. A responsive layout succeeds through its own compositional quality, not resemblance to the reference's coordinates.

## Review discipline

Judge consequential visible gaps, not gratuitous differences. Give each blocker a concrete correction linked to this rubric. Flat vector rendering is not automatically sufficient, and aesthetic defects are not automatically waived as subjective preferences. Conversely, do not reject a successful intentional adaptation solely because it differs from the square reference. Report both gates independently, with the affected concept and shape identified. Screenshot evidence supports visual conclusions only; it does not establish runtime correctness.

## Prioritized visible corrections

### P1 — Redraw the foliage, pots, and shelves at the quality their prominence demands

**Analog: wide, medium, full, near-square.** The leaves are swollen oval forms with blunt/polygonal contours and repeated directions. At full size they look like chunky folded cutouts, rather than the reference's lighter trailing vine. The pot's narrow, hard sloping silhouette and nearly rectangular shelf reinforce the generic ornament impression. Near-square makes this particularly conspicuous because the branch is large and pushed against the right edge.

Use smoother pointed leaf contours with more varied size, angle, curvature, and overlap; slender flowing stems; restrained vein and leaf-face shading. Make a believable fuller terracotta vessel with a clear lip, curved wall/base, warm volume, and contact shadow. Give the shelf a shallow wooden top/front and controlled edge light. The solution need not become photorealistic, but the visible shapes and modeling must feel carefully illustrated. Keep the plant's current supporting role and allow a deliberate edge crop; no need to copy the reference's coordinates.

**Botanical: wide, medium, tall, full, near-square.** The large sprig has only a few broad, densely packed blades. The stiff central line, abrupt leaf junctions, large faceted faces, and nearly repeated paired arrangements make it heavy and mechanical. Full and near-square are the clearest evidence; tall shows the same problem despite the appropriate bottom placement.

Redraw this as an airy branch with flowing thin stems, more natural direction changes, clean tapering tips, modest curvature, and varied broad/narrow leaves. Reduce the visual density around the lower cluster. Add fine tonal/vein variation without thick outlines or conspicuous folds. Preserve the warm serif clock's dominance. Do not add foliage back to minimum or strip merely to prove fidelity; their omission is appropriate.

**Flip: wide, medium, full, near-square.** Its sprig repeats the same heavy leaf construction, while the vessel reads as a small flat teardrop and the shelf as a bar. Full reveals the absence of convincing vase volume most clearly.

Use a lighter, finer branching sprig and a pale ceramic vessel with a coherent neck, shoulder, base, soft directional shading, and contact shadow. Give the shelf the same restrained material finish as Analog. Keep the ornament subordinate and its generous breathing room.

### P1 — Give Flip panels tangible material and a more convincing joint

**All Flip shapes; inspect full and near-square first.** The panels currently read mainly as softly shaded olive rounded rectangles with a thin horizontal line. There is some gradient and external shadow, but their upper/lower faces are insufficiently distinguished. The seam does not suggest a recessed joint, and the pale edge hinges feel applied rather than seated in a mechanism. This is a finish failure even where foliage is absent, including tall and strip.

Introduce restrained upper/lower tonal and light differences, a very small recessed seam shadow/highlight, a credible rounded panel edge/thickness, and integrated hinge recesses. A subtle nonrepeating matte surface variation would help. Keep all of this gentle: the reference is warm and tactile, not shiny plastic. Preserve the strong ivory digits and their current clear reading order. Tune the seam so it remains secondary to the digits.

### P1 — Give Landscape an illustrated surface and coherent light

**All Landscape shapes; full and near-square show the gap most clearly.** The olive and terracotta bands are large unmodulated fields, while the sun is a crisp simple disc. The silhouette system is readable, but the scene does not yet have the reference's softly textured painted/paper character. Wide and strip make the art island's upright left edge and stiff first hill especially visible; tall magnifies the sun into a conspicuously plain shape.

Add low-contrast, fine paper/paint variation and broad gentle tonal/light modulation to the hills and warm sky. Give the sun a softer luminous character, avoiding a harsh rim. Refine the wide/strip left hill entrance so the curve meets the split composition gracefully instead of looking like an abruptly cut standalone block. Retain smooth layered overlaps and calm horizons. The existing serif time, hierarchy, aspect-ratio-specific landscape allocation, and intentional negative space should remain.

### P2 — Rebalance Analog strip as one information group

**Analog strip only.** In the 1007×142 crop, the dial is around x300–418 and the date around x426–494. Their combined group centers around x397, while the card center is x503. The much larger empty area on the right lacks a compositional reason; the result looks parked left rather than deliberately placed.

Center the combined dial/date group as a unit, with its existing readable date relationship, or establish another clear balanced alignment. This is not a request to fill the entire strip: the small round dial naturally follows the available height, and calm side space is appropriate when balanced.

### P3 — Strengthen Botanical's type distinction when refining finish

**Most visible in full and near-square.** The present serif is elegant and the scale is confident, but it is noticeably finer and more Times-like than the reference's fuller warm terracotta display. It also closely shares Landscape's numeral language. A modestly stronger display weight and a slightly warmer copper hue would better establish Botanical's own identity. This is a refinement, not an independent blocker in the foliage-free minimum and strip crops.

## Successful choices to retain

- Large serif time in Landscape and Botanical is readable and visually confident. There is no general timid-type blocker in those concepts.
- Landscape's dates and AM/PM remain secondary; the tall variant's quiet sky is purposeful. Do not consume that space merely to imitate the square reference.
- Analog's tall dial-only layout is a valid calm composition. The dial and date remain legible; missing decoration there is acceptable.
- Flip's tall stack is an intentional response to its narrow shape. The digits remain easy to read and the labels are well attached.
- Botanical's asymmetric background shape and left alignment are valid adaptations. The organic background does not need to match the reference's exact blobs.
- No clipping, unreadable time, or label collision was observed in the supplied native crops. This does not establish refresh, animation, or time correctness.

## Actionable remaining blockers

### P1 / A1 — Analog vine lacks convincing botanical contours

**Affected: wide, medium, full, near-square.** Full and near-square provide the clearest native evidence. Several large leaves have nearly straight upper edges, blunt squared-off tips, abrupt planar corners, and rigid filled silhouettes. The larger upper-left and upper-right leaves are especially conspicuous. Fine stems and vein detail do not overcome these coarse primary contours. The result reads as a schematic leaf cutout rather than the reference's delicate trailing plant.

Redraw the leaf silhouettes themselves. Use varied, smooth heart/teardrop forms appropriate to a vine, with a definite natural tip, controlled concavity at the stem, and softly curved edges. Vary leaf turn, overlap, and shape rather than attaching repeated broad plaques. Apply quiet light/shadow across the leaf faces to suggest a slight curve, avoiding a single hard fold. Keep the pot, shelf, trailing direction, and current ornament allocation; this is an illustration correction, not a layout rebuild. Check full and near-square before judging the small crops.

### P1 / B1 — Botanical branch is too mechanical and heavy

**Affected: wide, medium, tall, full, near-square.** In full, the stem remains largely straight while the lower leaves form broad pointed wedges with abrupt corners and dense overlaps. Bare side lines protrude conspicuously beyond several leaf bases. Tall and near-square show the same stiff cluster even at smaller scale. Surface grain and fine veins supply detail, but the plant's silhouette still lacks the reference's airy curves and graceful varied foliage.

Draw a slightly curving central stem and naturally curved secondary stems. Attach leaves cleanly without exposed linear stubs. Give each leaf a more organically convex outline, curved tip, and distinct angle/length/width; open the lower cluster so it does not read as stacked triangular wedges. Use gentle leaf-face modeling rather than relying on the grain/veins for all depth. Preserve the large warm serif time and asymmetrical background; minimum and strip may continue omitting foliage.

### P1 / F1 — Flip ornament remains a paper silhouette rather than a modeled sprig and ceramic vase

**Affected: wide, medium, full, near-square.** Full is the strongest evidence. The sprig has hard pointed polygonal edges and abrupt leaf joins. The pale vessel's broad pear shape has almost no directional light or side/base definition; its visible surface reads as textured cream paper. The shelf has sufficient restrained thickness for its small role, but it cannot make the vase look volumetric.

Smooth and vary the sprig's leaf contours and curvature, retain fine branching, and give the foliage restrained leaf-face shading. Model the vase with a quiet lit side, shaded turning side, believable curved shoulder/neck/base, and a small contact shadow on the shelf. Avoid dark outlines or glossy plastic effects. The vessel should retain its pale ceramic character and modest size. No need to enlarge the decoration or move it closer to the numerals.

The panels themselves are not an independent blocker in this round's images: their restrained bevel, soft shadow, fine matte surface, visible seam, and integrated edge hardware establish plausible physical objects at native scale. Digits remain confident and legible, including the tall stack and compact strip.

### P2 / L1 — Landscape side transition looks like an artificial blurred curtain

**Affected: wide and strip only.** At the left edge of the artwork, a conspicuous vertical column blurs/fades both terracotta and olive layers into the clock's background. Wide shows this at roughly the middle of the card; strip repeats it before its long scene. It reads as a soft mask applied to a separately cropped illustration, not as a natural feature of the warm painted landscape. The scene and type allocation can remain split, but this visible treatment is insufficiently finished.

Replace the vertical mask with clean intentional geometry. For example, begin the foreground near the bottom at the left of the scene and let a smooth low crest rise to the right, using the same crisp-but-softly-lit painted edge treatment as the other hills. Alternatively, carry a low landscape sweep across the base with a quiet land entrance below the time. Preserve the balanced text/art allocation and the strong numerals. A more intense blur is not the correction.

The other five Landscape crops pass: the smooth layered curves, fine surface variation, warm sun, restrained olive/terracotta modeling, and clear serif hierarchy form a convincing warm illustrated scene. The deliberately large cropped sun and calm sky in tall are acceptable adaptations.

## Theme findings

- Typography, display scale, label hierarchy, composition, and illustration geometry remain coherent in every palette/mode crop. No label or time loses usable visual contrast.
- The palette adaptations retain muted domestic surfaces and controlled lighting. Linen carries the literal ivory/olive/copper warmth; the other established palettes retain the same calm material and illustration language through their own hues. This is acceptance of the rendered palette adaptations, not an exemption for arbitrary color substitutions.
- Analog's dial remains readable in dark modes; the darker shallow rim is understated but still defines the disc. Hands and cardinal marks remain visible. Plant and pot retain enough tonal modeling to read as illustrated objects.
- Flip maintains large clear digits, restrained seams, hardware, and panel depth. The pale-panel/dark-digit reversal in several dark modes is coherent and preserves the physical mechanism. The vase's volume remains visible in both polarities.
- Botanical's organic ground remains subordinate to the time in dark modes, and its foliage retains clean contours and separation. No branch becomes a dense illegible mass.
- Landscape retains distinct smooth layers and paper-like surface variation. Its dark-mode sun/disc is intentionally subdued but still discernible; it is decorative, and clock readability remains strong. Terrain light/dark reversals produce a coherent stylized scene without a clipping or hierarchy issue.
- Contrast variants preserve defined curves and materials while providing clear information hierarchy. No halo, unreadable seam, or harsh outline creates a consequential finish problem.
